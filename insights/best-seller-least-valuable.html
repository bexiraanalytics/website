PROFITABILITY · 3 MIN READ

Your best-selling product might be your least valuable.

Every catalog has a product everyone points to first — the one with the biggest sales numbers, the one that gets mentioned in the all-hands meeting. It's rarely the product actually worth the most attention, and treating "best-selling" and "most valuable" as the same thing is one of the more expensive mix-ups in retail and e-commerce.

Two different questions

"What sells the most?" and "what makes us the most money, per unit sold?" are different questions with different answers, and a business usually only has an easy answer to the first one. Revenue and unit volume are visible everywhere — in every sales report, every "top products" widget. Margin per product takes deliberate work to calculate and rarely gets the same visibility, even though it's the number that actually determines whether growing that product's sales helps or hurts the business.

What this looked like in practice

In one product-performance analysis, the single highest-profit item in the catalog (by absolute naira) and the single highest-margin item were two completely different products — the top seller converted at a markedly lower margin than a much smaller, less-discussed product elsewhere in the catalog. Scaling the "best seller" further would have scaled revenue. Scaling the high-margin product would have scaled profit. See how we found it →

A better question to ask your own catalog

Not "what are our top sellers" — but "if we could only double the sales of one product this quarter, which one would actually move profit the most?" The two lists are rarely identical, and the difference between them is usually where the real opportunity is sitting, unattended.
