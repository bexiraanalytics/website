Revenue went up. So why did profit fall?

A revenue chart going up and to the right is the easiest number in a business to feel good about. It's also, on its own, one of the least informative. Revenue tells you demand existed. It doesn't tell you what it cost to fill that demand — and that gap is where profit quietly disappears.

How the illusion forms

It usually isn't fraud or mismanagement. It's composition. As a business grows, its revenue mix shifts — a higher-volume, lower-margin category starts contributing a larger share of total sales, discounting creeps in to protect volume, or fulfillment and acquisition costs rise faster than prices do. Every one of those shows up as "more revenue" on a dashboard, and none of them show up unless someone is specifically looking at margin, not just top line.

A real example

In one e-commerce analysis we looked at, the highest-revenue product category was also, by a wide margin, the least profitable — converting sales into profit at roughly 14 points lower margin than the next category. Looked at by revenue alone, it was the star performer. Looked at by margin, it was the one quietly dragging overall profitability down. Read the full case study →

What to check before you trust a growth number

  • Is margin, not just revenue, growing at a similar rate?
  • Has the mix between your product categories or channels shifted recently?
  • Are discounts or acquisition costs higher than they were a year ago?
  • Would this quarter still look like growth if you measured it in profit instead of revenue?
